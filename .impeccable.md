## Design Context

### Users

**Primary:** LEGO investors and resellers — adults who treat LEGO sets as financial assets. They scan at thrift stores, garage sales, and estate sales looking for deals. They track portfolio value over time and want to know if a set is worth buying or selling *right now*.

**Context:** Used on mobile, often in a hurry. Quick scan → fast answer → confident decision. Not browsing leisurely — hunting.

**Job to be done:** "Tell me what this set is worth in the real market, instantly, so I can act."

### Brand Personality

**Bold, playful, energetic** — the data is serious (this is people's money) but the experience feels alive. Like LEGO itself: deliberately colorful, confident, never boring. Think of it as the intersection of a financial tool and a collector's obsession — not one or the other.

Three-word anchor: **Fast. Sharp. Alive.**

**Emotional goals:**
- Confidence: "I know exactly what this is worth"
- Delight: The price reveal should feel like opening a treasure chest
- Trust: Real data from real markets, cited clearly

### Aesthetic Direction

**Theme:** Dark. (#0e0e0e background) Users are scanning in varied environments — garage sales, attics, storage units. Dark reduces eye strain and keeps attention on the numbers.

**Accent:** LEGO yellow (#ffc32c) — used with restraint and precision. Not splashed everywhere. Reserved for CTAs, active states, and the moments that matter.

**Visual feel:** Somewhere between editorial magazine and financial dashboard. Tight type, sharp contrast, deliberate motion. Confident without being corporate. Fun without being childish.

**Reference energy:**
- cal.ai — tight button system, hover lift, loading states, onboarding polish
- Robinhood (early era) — financial data made beautiful and accessible
- Shiny TCG app — scanner UX, full-screen camera, "card detected" reveal patterns

### Anti-references (explicitly NOT this)

1. **Toy/kids app** — No rounded-everything, no bright-primary-color soup, no comic-sans energy. The subject is LEGO; the users are adults making financial decisions.
2. **Heavy e-commerce/marketplace** — Not BrickLink.com. No dense tables, no product grids, no utilitarian gray. That's where the data comes from, not what we aspire to look like.

### Design Principles

1. **Speed first.** Investors and resellers are in a hurry. Every interaction should feel instant. Loading states should be short-lived and purposeful. No decorative spinners.

2. **Trust through clarity.** Show the numbers prominently, cite sources, explain what "sold average" means. This is money. Ambiguity kills trust.

3. **LEGO energy, adult execution.** Bold color, sharp contrast, deliberate motion — playful without being juvenile. The yellow accent should feel like an exclamation point, not wallpaper.

4. **The reveal is everything.** The price count-up animation is the core emotional moment of the product. Every screen builds toward it. Don't let anything else compete with that beat.

5. **Not BrickLink, not a toy store.** Dense tables are lazy. Cartoon rounded UIs are wrong. Aim for editorial confidence — like a well-designed finance magazine that happens to cover LEGO.

### Accessibility

Best-effort (not formally audited): good contrast ratios, legible type at all sizes, tap targets minimum 44×44px. Reduced motion respected via `prefers-reduced-motion`.
