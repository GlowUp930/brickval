## Design Context

### Users

BrickVal serves LEGO collectors, buyers, and resellers who identify items, check current value, and maintain a collection from an iPhone. They often scan one-handed in shops, markets, collection rooms, or other visually busy environments, so the interface must remain fast, legible, and recoverable.

### Brand Personality

Confident, precise, and quietly playful. BrickVal should make market information feel trustworthy and premium while retaining the tactile satisfaction of collecting LEGO. It should never feel like a toy catalogue, a trading terminal, or a generic AI scanner.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use native iOS structure, restrained dark camera and result surfaces, real product imagery, and BrickVal green as a scarce functional accent. Motion should feel smooth and crafted, but every animation must explain detection, progress, transition, or confirmation. Avoid decorative glass, excessive cards, novelty effects, and marketing-style composition inside the app.

### Design Principles

- Keep the camera and the object being scanned visually primary.
- Make system state and the next available action understandable immediately.
- Present identity, condition, and market value with collector-grade clarity.
- Earn delight through precise feedback, sound, and motion rather than decoration.
- Preserve agency and accessibility through native controls, explicit recovery, Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, contrast, and Reduce Motion support.

The project design authority is [Apple HIG Rules for Codex](docs/design/APPLE_HIG_CODEX.md).
