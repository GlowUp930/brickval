# Adjacent utility and scanning app creative research

**Research date:** 2026-09-14  
**Question:** Which adjacent utility or scanning apps show sustained Meta Ad Library activity, and what creative and funnel patterns are worth testing for BrickValue?

## Scope and method

I checked Meta Ad Library with **Active ads**, **All regions**, and keyword searches on 2026-09-14. The searches were sorted by total impressions where Meta exposed that option. I then opened the exact advertiser pages when Meta offered an unambiguous match and verified the advertiser page name, destination, and first-party App Store or product site before treating an entry as relevant.

Keyword counts below are clearly labelled and can include unrelated pages. Where available, the stronger **exact advertiser-page count** is included as a separate snapshot. Meta's ordinary Ad Library view does not expose spend, return on ad spend, or conversion rate for these app ads. Active ads, repeated creative variants, and long run dates are useful signals of continued testing or delivery; they do not prove profitability. The official [Meta Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/) and [Ad Library API](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/api/) are the source of the live records.

## Strongest benchmarks

### CoinSnap — collectible identification and value

**Live signal.** The [CoinSnap keyword search](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&q=CoinSnap&search_type=keyword_unordered&sort_data%5Bdirection%5D=desc&sort_data%5Bmode%5D=total_impressions) showed approximately **1,500 keyword results**. The exact [CoinSnap advertiser page](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&sort_data%5Bdirection%5D=desc&sort_data%5Bmode%5D=total_impressions&view_all_page_id=100248529465493) showed approximately **640 active results** in the same snapshot. The visible relevant entries were all explicitly branded **CoinSnap** (`facebook.com/CoinSnapAI/`) and linked to CoinSnap's site or the App Store. Examples included library IDs `1457028688620726` (10 Apr 2026), `1928616794417823` (9 Feb 2026), `1946961982837394` (9 Aug 2026; nine ads using the same creative/text), `1465469488948599` (7 Aug 2026), and localized entries in Traditional Chinese and Italian.

**Creative patterns observed.**

- Curiosity and an asymmetric payoff: a small face-value coin could be worth much more. One short hook asked, “$1 or $1,000?”
- News-style urgency: a “BREAKING” frame introduced the possibility of a valuable ordinary coin.
- One clear action: take a photo, identify the coin, and see its value. The repeated CTA was equivalent to “scan before you spend.”
- Short vertical videos, roughly 23–34 seconds in the visible examples.
- The same core creative was localized rather than merely translated in the store listing; Italian and Traditional Chinese variants were visible in the active results.

**First-party product context.** Apple's [CoinSnap listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/coinsnap-coin-identifier/id1634551626) showed 301K ratings at 4.7, a collection/value proposition, 300,000+ coin types and 99% recognition claims, and English plus 19 additional languages. These are developer claims shown by Apple, not independently audited results.

**What this suggests for BrickValue.** The transferable idea is the **value question**, not the claim that an item will be rare or worth a fortune. A BrickValue version should show a real LEGO image, the identified set or minifigure, the New/Used state, and the estimated market value sourced from the app. Use wording such as “Could this be worth more than you paid?” or “What is this LEGO worth today?” and keep “estimated” and sold/listing source labels visible. Do not promise a price before the scan.

### Cal AI — photo utility with a paid result

**Live signal.** The [Cal AI keyword search](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&q=Cal%20AI&search_type=keyword_unordered&sort_data%5Bdirection%5D=desc&sort_data%5Bmode%5D=total_impressions) showed approximately **4,500 keyword results**. The exact [Cal AI advertiser page](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&sort_data%5Bdirection%5D=desc&sort_data%5Bmode%5D=total_impressions&view_all_page_id=311353912066626) showed approximately **830 active results**. The relevant records were explicitly from **Cal AI** (`facebook.com/getcalai/`). The visible library IDs included `965433192736864`, `1986351665424191`, `981785190979057`, `1500152458418243`, `2048808576029783`, `1314856373497809`, `2922821464729858`, and `3226056611116939`; the records showed seven or eight ads reusing a creative/text combination and start dates from April through June 2026.

**Creative patterns observed.**

- The promise is reduced effort: “Track your calories as easy as taking a picture of your food.”
- The visual sequence is one input (a meal photo) followed by an immediate, specific output (calories and nutrients).
- Longer versions explain the benefit without adding a new product idea; visible durations ranged from 9 to 39 seconds.
- Copy emphasizes removing manual work and saving time. This is a strong fit for BrickValue's “scan a lot instead of typing set numbers” story.

**First-party product context.** Cal AI's [official site](https://calai.app/) says “Track your calories with just a picture” and highlights photo/barcode input and automatic nutrient output. Apple's [Cal AI listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/cal-ai-calorie-tracker/id6480417616?platform=ipad) showed 362K ratings at 4.8, English plus 14 additional languages, and explicitly states that food-scanning analysis results require a subscription. The subscription gate is a product decision to test, not evidence that BrickValue should copy it unchanged.

**What this suggests for BrickValue.** Make the ad's proof moment concrete: phone points at a figure or set, the result names it, then the price card reveals sold history and collection value. The first frame should communicate one action and one payoff. A good 8–12 second structure is: `photo → recognition → New/Used price → “Scan yours”`. Bulk scanning can use the same structure with a visible lot count and a total value, while keeping individual prices readable.

### Rock Identifier — repeated creative, localization, and store handoff

**Live signal.** The [Rock Identifier keyword search](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&q=Rock%20Identifier&search_type=keyword_unordered&sort_data%5Bdirection%5D=desc&sort_data%5Bmode%5D=total_impressions) showed approximately **1,100 keyword results**. The exact [Rock Identifier advertiser page](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&sort_data%5Bdirection%5D=desc&sort_data%5Bmode%5D=total_impressions&view_all_page_id=101604918486186) showed approximately **1,000 active results**. The visible relevant ads were explicitly from **Rock Identifier** (`facebook.com/RockIdentifierAI/`) and linked to the App Store or Google Play. At least 16 active records were visible in the first loaded results, including library IDs `1931068674279553`, `27019216874440078`, `2176108286561481`, `1775231720127410`, `1242921674456379`, `1705905227327902`, `1669258727690785`, and `1377248341287917`. They reused the same central promise across 19–37 second videos, with variants starting from June through August 2026.

**Creative patterns observed.**

- Very plain language: “Instantly identify stones, crystals, gems, minerals … by a snap.”
- The product category is repeated in the first sentence, so the user understands the utility without context.
- The advertiser reuses a winning concept across many durations and languages, including Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, and Traditional Chinese.
- The destination is a direct store handoff with an install-oriented CTA.

**First-party product context.** Apple's [Rock Identifier listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rock-identifier-stone-id/id1546796934?platform=ipad) presents photo identification, detailed profiles, and collection/knowledge features. It showed 80K ratings at 4.7 and English plus 13 additional languages.

**What this suggests for BrickValue.** Keep the first sentence literal and category-specific: “Scan a LEGO minifigure. See what it sells for.” Then make the app screen supply the proof. Create language versions of the hook and result labels for the markets BrickValue already supports instead of using an English-only ad with a translated store page.

### Ludex — closest collector/value funnel

**Live signal.** The [Ludex keyword search](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&q=Ludex&search_type=keyword_unordered&sort_data%5Bdirection%5D=desc&sort_data%5Bmode%5D=total_impressions) showed approximately **170 keyword results**. The exact [Ludex advertiser page](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&is_targeted_country=false&media_type=all&search_type=page&sort_data%5Bdirection%5D=desc&sort_data%5Bmode%5D=total_impressions&view_all_page_id=100223492626391) showed approximately **35 active results**. The relevant records were explicitly from **Ludex: Sports + TCG Card Scanner App** (`facebook.com/LudexApp/`). More than a dozen visible active ads used different versions of the same scanner/value promise. Examples included library IDs `985969081077291` and `1294587356185491` (both “Snap a photo. Get instant card values.” in 14–16 second videos), `2359820851177634` (a 14 second “take a picture” explanation), `959777163716975` (a 14 second collection/value message), `974861891924661` (a 35 second variant), `1031112052911543`, `1677680066984387`, `1643112052911543`, `4187593411543861`, and `1702541897634380`. Active start dates ranged from April through September 2026.

**Creative patterns observed.**

- Very direct hook: “Snap a photo. Get instant card values.”
- The ad often names all three jobs: scan, value, and build/manage a collection.
- The CTA uses “free” or “start scanning” to lower the first-action barrier.
- Several durations (14, 22, 36, and 45 seconds) carry the same core claim, which is a practical way to test hooks without rebuilding the entire demo.
- One visible card-industry partnership ad offered $25 off a first PSA submission, showing that partner benefits can support acquisition when they are relevant to the collector's next action.

**First-party product context.** Ludex's [official site](https://www.ludex.com/) says users can scan, organize, buy and sell cards, and that prices are based on actual sales. Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ludex-sports-card-scanner-tcg/id1616691213) showed 27K ratings at 4.7 and a tiered funnel: free scanning/collection capacity, then paid tiers with price reports and listing limits, including a seven-day trial for paid plans. The listing says the free membership allows unlimited scans and up to 60 cards; those limits and prices can change, so treat them as a snapshot.

**What this suggests for BrickValue.** This is the best benchmark for the collector journey: `scan → value → collection → selling/buying decision`. A BrickValue ad should show a real decision that the price data resolves: “Should I keep, buy, or sell this?” The result should distinguish sold data from active listings and show the condition, seller-country context, and currency so the claim is credible.

## The reusable creative framework

The observed pattern can be reduced to **Snap → Proof → Payoff → Next action**. This is an inference from the live examples above, not a claim that the examples share a public framework.

1. **Snap:** show the physical object and one simple action in the first second. Use a tight, vertical shot with the app UI visible.
2. **Proof:** show the actual recognition result quickly. For BrickValue, include the set/minifigure name and condition rather than a generic “AI found it” animation.
3. **Payoff:** show a trustworthy price signal: completed-sale average or range, time window, currency, and a collection total for bulk scans. Keep “estimated market value” visible where appropriate.
4. **Next action:** ask for the user's immediate question: “Scan yours,” “Check before you buy,” “See what your collection is worth,” or “Scan a whole lot.”

For a 9–12 second vertical ad, use approximately `0–1s hook / 1–4s scan / 4–8s result / 8–12s CTA`. Produce several versions from the same screen recording by changing only the opening hook, object, condition, and voiceover. That follows the repeated-creative pattern seen in CoinSnap, Cal AI, Rock Identifier, and Ludex and keeps production cost low.

Meta's [Reels guidance](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads) reports better results in its own tests for 9:16 video with audio and key messages in the safe zone, and recommends testing variants. Use subtitles for muted playback, keep text away from the Reels UI, and prepare one clean vertical master before adapting it to other placements.

## BrickValue creative test slate

Start with five concepts, each with three opening hooks. Keep the middle proof sequence and CTA consistent so the first read is about the hook.

| Concept | Opening hook options | Proof sequence | Best audience intent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Value curiosity** | “What is this LEGO worth?” / “Could this minifigure be worth more than you paid?” / “Before you sell it, check this.” | Photo → identity → estimated New/Used value → source/time window | Casual owners and gift recipients |
| **Collection lot** | “Scan a whole LEGO lot in seconds.” / “What is this box of minifigures worth?” / “Stop typing set numbers one by one.” | Bulk photo → individual prices → total lot value → add to collection | Active collectors |
| **Buy/sell decision** | “Is this a good buy?” / “Check the used price before you pay.” / “What could this sell for today?” | Photo → condition → sold data versus active listings → market change | Buyers and sellers |
| **History proof** | “See what LEGO actually sold for.” / “Track the last 6 months.” / “Is your collection rising?” | Item → dated graph → selected timeframe → current owned quantity | Portfolio-minded users |
| **Discovery/story** | “Which LEGO character is this?” / “Found this in an old box?” / “Identify your childhood collection.” | Photo → name/theme/year → value → add to collection | Broad discovery traffic |

Do not show an inflated single price as if it were guaranteed sale proceeds. When listing fallback data is used, label it as active asking prices. When history is sparse, show the coverage or empty state in the product screen and avoid using that result as the ad's central proof.

## Funnel and measurement plan

The success metric should be **paid value per acquired user**, not the number of installs or cheap trials alone. Keep PostHog, RevenueCat, and App Store Connect joined by a stable anonymous install ID or account ID, with no email, receipt, or payment data in ad events.

Capture these dimensions on every acquisition and monetization event where available: `campaign_id`, `adset_id`, `creative_id`, `hook_id`, `locale`, `storefront`, `placement`, `app_build`, and `scan_mode`. Use Meta's campaign/creative identifiers or a controlled deep-link parameter; do not rely on the ad's headline as an identifier.

Measure this funnel by creative and country:

`ad click → install/app_ready → onboarding_started → first_scan_started → scan_completed → result_viewed → collection_add or bulk_add → upgrade_requested → paywall_presented → trial_started → paid/renewed at 7 and 30 days`.

The first decision gate is `first_scan_completed / app_ready`; the revenue gate is `net proceeds at 30 days / install`. Include refunds, cancellations, trial-to-paid conversion, and scan failure rate. A creative that produces a high click-through rate but low first-scan completion or high immediate cancellation is not a winner.

Run one controlled change at a time:

- Keep the same audience and store page while testing hooks.
- Keep the same hook while testing free-first-scan, no-trial, and a low-cost introductory offer. The offer must be evaluated on 30-day net revenue and retention, not trial starts.
- Keep the same creative while testing a result-page upgrade prompt after the price reveal versus a pre-scan paywall. The aim is to charge after the user has received credible value while protecting scan-cost margins.
- Review creative survival, first-scan conversion, paid conversion, and cancellation weekly. Retire variants on poor downstream economics, not simply because a newer ad has more clicks.

## Decisions and next actions

1. Build the first three BrickValue ads around the value-curiosity, lot-value, and buy/sell concepts. Use real product captures from the current build and clearly label the data source.
2. Produce each as a 9:16 vertical master with captions, then cut 9, 15, and 30 second versions. Use the same result sequence so the test isolates the opening hook.
3. Localize the best hook/result pair for the supported high-value storefronts. CoinSnap and Rock Identifier show that localized creative is a repeatable paid-social pattern, while the App Store listings show that language coverage can be broad.
4. Add creative and campaign dimensions to the existing analytics events before scaling spend. Build one PostHog dashboard that reports install-to-first-scan, first-scan-to-upgrade, trial-to-paid, cancellation, and 30-day net revenue by `hook_id`.
5. Run a small test budget until at least one concept wins on paid value per install. Meta Ad Library activity is a source of hypotheses; BrickValue's own cohort economics decide what to scale.

## Limits

- Meta's public library does not give reliable spend, impression, or ROAS data for these ordinary ads. “Massive in Meta ads” here means a large visible active keyword footprint or many repeated relevant creatives, not verified budget.
- Search counts can include unrelated advertisers. Relevant entries were counted only when the advertiser page matched the app.
- Dates and counts are a snapshot and will change. Recheck the saved links before making a budget decision.
- App Store ratings, downloads, recognition rates, and user totals are the publishers' or Apple's displayed values; they are evidence of market presence, not proof of ad profitability or accuracy.
- Creative examples are references for structure and testing. BrickValue should use its own data, screenshots, brand, and legally accurate claims.
